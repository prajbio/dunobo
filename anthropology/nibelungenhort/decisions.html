Turn uncertainty into explicit questions

Tests, decisions and probabilities

The useful question is not just “where should we search?” It is “what observation would change our view, and could this method reliably produce it?”

Define three different outcomes

Discover a deposit

Recover and authenticate an ancient hoard with documented context.

Support a historical association

Use dating, material study and context to assess a possible Burgundian link.

Identify the legendary treasure

Require exceptional independent evidence beyond location, age and precious metal.

These are progressively stronger claims. The calculations below concern a hypothesised underlying hoard, not proof of its literary identity.

What does a negative search tell you?

Define the hypothesis as a recoverable target somewhere in a specified region. Coverage is the share of its location probability actually investigated, not simply the percentage of land area walked.

18%Effective chance of finding a present target
8.35%Remaining probability after a negative result
q = coverage × detection; P(target | negative) = p(1 − q) / (1 − pq)

Simplified model: one target hypothesis, correct coverage weighting, and no falsely authenticated discoveries. For heterogeneous areas, integrate location weights and depth-specific detection. Repeated surveys with shared blind spots are not independent. Impossible input combinations are flagged. E42 Negative evidence needs a defined detection probability

Decision rules for further work

  1. Recover the original evidence. A traceable sample or campaign log can justify a narrowly scoped re-examination.
  2. Locate and date the landscape. Do not transfer a medieval name or modern river bend directly into the fifth century.
  3. Validate the method. Test realistic target forms and blank controls. Report uncertainty in the detection rate.
  4. Examine promising anomalies independently. An anomaly is not a treasure identification.
  5. Stop when the claim fails its test. Retain “unresolved” where inaccessible ground or poor penetration prevents a fair test.

Before fieldwork, agree the archaeological and waterway permissions, access, conservation and custody arrangements with the responsible bodies. S31 S32 S34 S39

What would materially change the assessment?

Evidence that could strengthen a candidate

A documented ancient concentration of worked precious metal; preserved depositional context; verified dating; a reproducible subsurface target; or an independent historical record tied to a precise place.

Evidence that could weaken or retire it

Modern material explaining an anomaly; a mislocated or misdated source; demonstrated removal of the relevant deposits; or adequate negative coverage for a predeclared target class.
