01 / The foundations

Life is quantum.
That is the starting point,
not the explanation.

Chemical bonds, molecular energy levels and light absorption depend on quantum mechanics. The useful question is which quantum features must remain explicit to explain a biological observation.[1]

Quantum biology asks how effects such as tunneling, excitation coherence and electron-spin dynamics influence biological processes. A local quantum event can feed into a larger system that is well described by ordinary reaction rates.[4]

Keep three claims separate: an effect occurs; it changes function; it provides an adaptive advantage.

01

Occurrence

Can we detect a defined quantum feature?
Example: a tunneling contribution to a reaction.

02

Functional contribution

Does changing that feature alter a biological outcome, with alternative explanations controlled?

03

Adaptive advantage

Does it improve performance relative to a realistic alternative—and is there evidence of evolutionary selection?

Two views of the mechanism

Different levels of description. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [4][6][61][62]

k_ET ∝ |V_DA|² × nuclear reorganization factor

How the mechanism works

An electron changes redox site without requiring the entire protein to conduct like a metal. Coupling decreases strongly with separation and depends on molecular pathways. Surrounding nuclei reorganize, so thermally activated motion and quantum transfer cooperate. A sequence of localized transfers can carry a sustained biological current. [4][6]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Distance- and energetics-dependent protein electron-transfer measurements support tunneling-based rate models. The nonadiabatic Marcus limit relates rates to coupling, free-energy difference and reorganization energy; it is an approximation, not a universal equation for every complex. [4][6][61][62]

Where the evidence stops

A tunneling step does not establish coherent motion across an entire respiratory chain. Nor does it show that tunneling alone accounts for an enzyme’s catalytic enhancement relative to a suitable reference reaction. [4][6][61][62]

Biological roleEstablished redox function
Advantage / adaptationOptimization is system-specific

Real-world relevance

Established scientific foundation

Guides interpretation of respiratory biochemistry and design of redox enzymes and bioelectrodes. A new electrode or catalyst still needs direct efficiency, stability and lifetime measurements. This is not a general mitochondrial treatment claim.

02 / Energy & catalysisEstablished

Hydrogen tunneling in enzymes

Substantial contributions in selected reactions

Hydrogen-atom, hydride and proton-transfer reactions; lipoxygenase and alcohol dehydrogenase are landmark systems.

Two views of the mechanism

Classical limit versus explicit nuclear dynamics. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. Rectangular barrier is a teaching model, not an enzyme calculation. [5][21]

T ∝ exp[−2a√(2m(V−E))/ℏ]

How the mechanism works

A protein samples configurations that bring the reacting groups into suitable geometries. Hydrogen transfer can then have a substantial nuclear-tunneling contribution. The transferred chemical species depends on the enzyme: a proton, hydride and hydrogen atom are not interchangeable labels. [5][21]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Enzyme isotope experiments, including a very large hydrogen/deuterium kinetic isotope effect in soybean lipoxygenase, support this mechanism when combined with temperature and mutation studies. Early alcohol-dehydrogenase work also established a major experimental route into the field. [5][21]

Where the evidence stops

A kinetic isotope effect by itself is not a unique tunneling signature: zero-point energies, conformational changes and kinetic masking matter. Tunneling in an enzyme does not automatically explain its advantage over the corresponding solution reaction. [5][21]

Physical effectStrong in selected enzymes
Biological roleContributes to reaction rates
Advantage / adaptationNot a universal catalytic explanation

Real-world relevance

Established mechanism; design case by case

Useful for mechanistic enzymology and rational biocatalyst design. Isotope substitution can alter metabolic stability, but a useful deuterated molecule must be evaluated on its own chemistry and pharmacology; “more tunneling” is not a drug-development objective.

A discriminating next test · proposedUse several isotopes, temperature dependence, intrinsic chemical-step kinetics, structural checks and reference reactions. Perturb donor–acceptor distance without simply destroying activity.

03 / Energy & catalysisEstablished

Proton-coupled electron transfer

Coupled charge chemistry; details depend on pathway

Radical enzymes, including ribonucleotide reductase, which supplies deoxyribonucleotide precursors.

Two views of the mechanism

Different levels of description. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [23][4][5]

Electron coordinate + proton coordinate + protein environment

How the mechanism works

PCET avoids highly unfavorable isolated charge states by coordinating electronic and protonic changes. A radical relay can span several residues while individual proton movements remain local. “Coupled” does not require an electron and a proton to travel the same long distance or to move simultaneously in every mechanism. [23][4]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

A trapped ribonucleotide-reductase holocomplex reveals an organized radical-transfer pathway. Together with redox and isotope-based mechanistic approaches, such structural information constrains how a protein controls reactive intermediates. [23][4][5]

Where the evidence stops

A structure establishes geometry, not a measured nuclear tunneling probability. One should distinguish proof of a PCET reaction scheme from proof that a particular proton tunnels or that long-lived coherence drives the full radical relay. [23][4][5]

Physical effectCoupled chemistry established
Biological roleEssential in characterized enzymes
Advantage / adaptationQuantum-specific advantage not isolated

Real-world relevance

Established biochemical relevance

Offers targets for enzyme inhibition and design principles for catalysts that manage coupled charges. Predicting an inhibitor’s selectivity still requires conventional biochemical, cellular and safety testing.

A discriminating next test · proposedCombine site-specific redox substitutions, solvent isotope effects, time-resolved radical detection and calculations that compare sequential and concerted pathways.

04 / Genome & repairEstablished

DNA photolyase repair

Light-driven electron chemistry repairs a lesion

Photolyases in organisms that perform light-dependent repair of ultraviolet-induced DNA lesions.

Two views of the mechanism

Different levels of description. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [22][4]

FAD* → lesion reduction → bond splitting → electron return

How the mechanism works

A cyclobutane pyrimidine dimer links adjacent pyrimidines. Excitation of the enzyme’s cofactor initiates electron transfer that changes the lesion’s chemical reactivity; the damaged bonds can split and the catalytic redox state is restored. Charge transfer, nuclear motion and electron return are distinct steps. [22][4]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Ultrafast experiments resolve the sequence of electronic and bond-rearrangement events in CPD photolyase. This is a mechanistically detailed example of quantum photochemistry accomplishing a biological task, rather than evidence inferred solely from an abstract model. [22][4]

Where the evidence stops

The existence of photolyase in one organism cannot be generalized to every species or to an effective treatment in human skin. A microscopic repair mechanism and the delivery/efficacy of a topical product are separate questions. [22][4]

Physical effectStrong molecular evidence
Biological roleDirect lesion repair demonstrated
Advantage / adaptationEvolutionary optimization not separately quantified

Real-world relevance

Established biology; translation requires testing

Informs DNA-damage biology, photochemical catalyst design and evaluation of repair-inspired technologies. Product or clinical efficacy is not established merely by demonstrating electron transfer in a purified enzyme.

A discriminating next test · proposedAlter cofactor–lesion geometry or electronic energetics and measure both intermediate lifetimes and completed repair, controlling binding and protein integrity.

05 / Light & photochemistryEstablished

Vision and retinal photochemistry

A local quantum event initiates visual signaling

The first light-sensitive step in rhodopsin and related retinal-binding photoproteins.

Two views of the mechanism

Different levels of description. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [3]

Photon → excited-state dynamics → isomer → signaling

How the mechanism works

Absorption changes the electronic state of the retinal chromophore. Nuclear motion on coupled potential-energy surfaces enables rapid isomerization. Protein surroundings shape competing relaxation routes and the probability of a productive photoproduct. Later signaling can be described by ordinary biochemical amplification. [3]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Femtosecond measurements of rhodopsin established approximately 200-femtosecond initial isomerization. Quantum photochemistry is needed for this local event; its speed and specificity can be studied without assuming a globally coherent nervous system. [3]

Where the evidence stops

The quantum character of photon absorption does not imply entangled visual perception or quantum consciousness. Different opsins have different photocycles, so this diagram is not a universal time-resolved map of every photoreceptor. [3]

Physical effectStrong
Biological roleTriggers visual receptor response
Advantage / adaptationMechanistic tuning; no global quantum claim

Real-world relevance

Established photochemistry

Supports photoreceptor research and design of light-responsive molecular switches. Engineered light-control tools must be evaluated on wavelength, efficiency, kinetics and phototoxicity—not a generic claim of quantum superiority.

A discriminating next test · proposedMeasure excited-state lifetimes, product yield and subsequent receptor activation after a chromophore or binding-pocket change; separate photophysics from downstream signaling.

06 / Light & photochemistryEstablished

Photosynthetic charge separation

Light energy creates separated redox charges

Photosynthetic reaction centers, including photosystem II.

Two views of the mechanism

Different levels of description. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [30][6]

Excitation energy ≠ charge separation

How the mechanism works

The primary photochemistry converts excitation into a redox separation that can feed downstream chemistry. This is different from moving excitation energy between antenna pigments: an electron changes site and a positive counterpart remains. Protein electrostatics and nuclear motion affect the available routes. [30][6]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Multispectral two-dimensional spectroscopy has resolved early charge-separation dynamics in isolated photosystem II reaction centers. These data constrain assignments of excited and charge-transfer states and their coupling. [30][6]

Where the evidence stops

Competing assignments of the earliest intermediates do not call the existence of charge separation into question. Nor does demonstrating a quantum charge-transfer mechanism establish that long-lived antenna coherence provides an organism-level efficiency advantage. [30][6]

Physical effectStrong for charge separation
Biological roleCentral energy-conversion step
Advantage / adaptationSpecific enhancement claims require comparison

Real-world relevance

Established natural function; engineered analogues

A scientific foundation for artificial photosynthetic reaction centers and photoredox materials. Solar-device performance additionally depends on absorption, charge extraction, stability and losses beyond the first transfer event.

A discriminating next test · proposedUse multiple spectral observables and selective pigment perturbations to distinguish charge-transfer populations from overlapping exciton and vibrational signals.

07 / Light & photochemistryEstablished

Excitons and resonance energy transfer

Coupled pigments; coherent and incoherent limits

Light-harvesting antennas and donor–acceptor fluorescence systems.

Two views of the mechanism

Different levels of description. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [29][31]

Weak-coupling limit: k_FRET ∝ (R₀/R)⁶

How the mechanism works

Resonance energy transfer moves electronic excitation without necessarily moving a net charge between molecules. At weak coupling and suitable relaxation times, transfer is well represented by rates; stronger coupling can require a delocalized excitonic description. Different parts of one complex may occupy different regimes. [29][31]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Förster theory and modern spectroscopy provide complementary descriptions. Electronic–vibrational studies of light-harvesting complexes resolve couplings beyond a simple picture of independent pigments. [29][31]

Where the evidence stops

FRET is not a non-quantum alternative to quantum physics. Spatial delocalization does not by itself establish long-lived electronic coherence, and a quantum-state description is not proof of evolutionary advantage. [29][31]

Physical effectStrong for molecular coupling
Biological roleEnergy transfer established
Advantage / adaptationLong-lived coherence advantage is separate

Real-world relevance

Established research principle

Resonance transfer underpins molecular-distance and interaction reporters; coupled-pigment studies also guide light-harvesting materials. Accurate reporter calibration remains essential because orientation and photophysics influence interpretation.

A discriminating next test · proposedVary pigment separation and spectral overlap; compare measured transfer with the weak-coupling rate limit and more complete open-system models.

08 / Light & photochemistryDebated

Long-lived coherence in photosynthesis

Persistence and functional advantage depend on system

The FMO complex and other light-harvesting assemblies; not one universal photosynthesis mechanism.

Two views of the mechanism

Competing dynamical explanations. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [7][8][9]

ρ off-diagonal terms may affect observables; basis and timescale matter

How the mechanism works

The proposed stronger claim is that persistent electronic coherence changes useful energy transfer under biological illumination. Electronic, vibrational and mixed vibronic coherences can produce different but overlapping spectroscopic signatures. Preparing an oscillation with a laser is not the same experiment as demonstrating a natural functional benefit. [7][8]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Engel’s 2007 study stimulated the field. Polarization-sensitive measurements in 2018 assigned long-lived FMO oscillations largely to vibrations and short-lived electronic coherence. Detailed 2025 simulations predicted persistent excitonic contributions, illustrating sensitivity to environmental modeling. [7][8][9]

Where the evidence stops

The newer simulation does not reverse earlier experiments by itself. Neither a spectroscopic beat nor a model’s transport yield proves an adaptive advantage in a living organism. [7][8][9]

Physical effectCoherences observed; assignments vary
Biological roleSpecific functional benefit debated
Advantage / adaptationNot established as a general adaptation

Real-world relevance

Research-stage design hypothesis

Can inspire controlled tests in light-harvesting materials. Claims that plants perform a quantum search or that a solar cell will improve simply by lengthening coherence go beyond the evidence.

A discriminating next test · proposedSelectively modify dephasing or vibronic resonance while preserving pigment energetics and structure; measure energy delivery and photochemistry under physiologically relevant preparation.

09 / Light & photochemistrySupported

Vibronic assistance

Electronic–vibrational mixing in selected samples

Natural light-harvesting complexes and engineered pigment aggregates.

Two views of the mechanism

Refinement of coupled-state dynamics. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [31][10][8]

Electronic excitation + vibrational quantum → mixed vibronic state

How the mechanism works

When a vibrational energy is close to an electronic energy separation, mixing can alter state character and transport. This is more specific than asserting that any long-lived oscillation enhances photosynthesis. The relevant quantity is a change in an observable pathway or yield, not coherence lifetime alone. [31][10]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

A 2020 study interpreted light-harvesting-complex-II spectroscopy in terms of vibronically facilitated energy flow. A 2026 study demonstrated functional vibronic coupling in disordered porphyrin nanotube aggregates at room temperature; those aggregates were artificial materials. [31][10][8]

Where the evidence stops

Evidence in one protein complex or synthetic aggregate is not proof that every antenna uses the same mechanism. Uniqueness of spectroscopic assignments and a realistic baseline still matter. [31][10][8]

Physical effectSupported in characterized samples
Biological rolePathway effects supported in samples
Advantage / adaptationGenerality and adaptation unresolved

Real-world relevance

Laboratory mechanism / materials design

Provides a design variable for artificial antennas and organic photonic materials. It is a plausible route to better transfer pathways, not a demonstrated universal efficiency upgrade.

A discriminating next test · proposedTune a vibrational mode with isotopic substitution or a designed analogue, then check transfer while independently monitoring energy levels, morphology and environmental changes.

10 / Light & photochemistrySupported

Environment-assisted quantum transport

Established model effect; natural optimization unproven

Open-system transport models and engineered quantum networks; proposed relevance to light harvesting.

Two views of the mechanism

Coherent-only baseline versus open-system dynamics. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [11][12]

Too little noise ← possible optimum → too much noise

How the mechanism works

The environment can help as well as hinder transport. This is a conditional result: its sign and magnitude depend on couplings, disorder, losses, preparation and what counts as successful transfer. The word “noise” does not specify one universal physical mechanism. [11][12]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Theoretical work demonstrated regimes of noise-enhanced transport. A ten-qubit engineered network provided an experimental realization of environment-assisted transfer, outside a natural photosynthetic organism. [11][12]

Where the evidence stops

A demonstration in an engineered network is not proof that a protein has evolved to its optimal dephasing rate. Some transport enhancement can also occur in classical stochastic models, so the comparator must be explicit. [11][12]

Physical effectStrong in specified models/platforms
Biological roleNatural contribution is conditional
Advantage / adaptationOptimal biological tuning unproven

Real-world relevance

Models and engineered proof of principle

Useful for engineered transport networks and for avoiding the false design rule “eliminate all noise.” A biological or photovoltaic benefit must be demonstrated in the intended device.

A discriminating next test · proposedConstruct a matched baseline and vary environmental coupling independently. Measure transfer probability, speed and loss, rather than selecting whichever metric improves.

11 / Light & photochemistryEstablished

Excited-state proton transfer in GFP

Proton transfer established; tunneling fraction model-dependent

Green fluorescent protein and related proton-sensitive fluorescent proteins.

Two views of the mechanism

Different levels of description. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [24][25]

Excitation → proton redistribution → emissive-state population

How the mechanism works

After excitation, the chromophore can transfer a proton through its local hydrogen-bond network. The emitting state and fluorescence response depend on that chemistry. Nuclear quantum calculations may change predicted transfer dynamics, but evidence for transfer and evidence for a particular tunneling contribution are different claims. [24][25]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Ultrafast vibrational spectroscopy directly tracks excited-state proton-transfer signatures. Explicit quantum-dynamical modeling has explored how the surrounding proton-transfer network shapes the process. [24][25]

Where the evidence stops

This is not proof of coherent proton conduction throughout an entire cell. Calculated populations depend on the included coordinates, potential-energy surfaces and treatment of the remaining protein. [24][25]

Physical effectStrong for excited-state proton transfer
Biological roleControls fluorescence pathways
Advantage / adaptationSeparate quantum advantage not demonstrated

Real-world relevance

Established reporter photochemistry

Informs fluorescent-protein engineering and interpretation of pH- or environment-sensitive reporters. Molecular brightness alone is insufficient: maturation, stability, blinking and cellular perturbation also matter.

12 / Genome & repairEstablished

Ultrafast nucleobase photoprotection

Nonradiative excited-state pathways

Isolated nucleobases and nucleic-acid photophysics, with routes altered by pairing, stacking and solvent.

Two views of the mechanism

Different levels of description. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [26][22]

Electronic energy → nuclear motion / heat, or competing photochemistry

How the mechanism works

Ultraviolet absorption prepares an electronic excitation. Nonradiative routes can divert population away from damaging chemistry, while competing pathways can produce lesions. An excited-state transition is not automatically a complete return to the ground state. [26][22]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Element-specific ultrafast measurements in thymine resolve a rapid ππ* to nπ* conversion. Such state-sensitive measurements improve on assigning all decay to one generic “photoprotection” lifetime. [26][22]

Where the evidence stops

A gas-phase or isolated-base result cannot be copied unchanged into duplex DNA. Fast decay can be protective, but evolutionary selection of the exact quantum pathway is a further inference. [26][22]

Physical effectStrong for specific ultrafast channels
Biological rolePhotostability relevance; context-dependent
Advantage / adaptationHistorical selection is an inference

Real-world relevance

Established photophysics; applied design relevance

Helps explain UV damage and informs photostable molecular design. It does not imply that DNA is immune to sunlight or that a modeled pathway validates a sunscreen.

A discriminating next test · proposedCompare state-resolved lifetimes and lesion yields in matched bases, nucleosides and stacked sequences; check whether changing one pathway changes damage probability.

13 / Light & photochemistryEstablished

Bioluminescence and chemiexcitation

Chemistry populates an emissive electronic state

Luciferin/luciferase systems and other biological light-producing reactions.

Two views of the mechanism

Rate description versus underlying quantum chemistry. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [27][1]

Chemical energy → emitter* → emitter + hν

How the mechanism works

Bioluminescence illustrates a broad definition of quantum biology: quantized electronic states link chemical energy to emitted light. The protein environment can alter the emitter’s energy levels, competing nonradiative pathways and color. Different organisms use distinct emitters and reaction chemistry. [27][1]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Firefly-oxyluciferin model spectroscopy tests how the local environment modulates emission. It supports an electronic-structure account of color while not settling every proposed chemical intermediate in every luciferase system. [27][1]

Where the evidence stops

Ordinary photon emission is not evidence for coherent quantum communication. Light yield, fluorescence quantum yield and overall bioluminescent reaction yield are related but distinct measures. [27][1]

Physical effectStrong electronic-emission basis
Biological roleBiological light production established
Advantage / adaptationNot a quantum-computing claim

Real-world relevance

Established assay principle

Provides a foundation for light-emitting biochemical reporters and assay design. The usefulness of a reporter depends on background, substrate supply, tissue transmission and measurement calibration.

A discriminating next test · proposedCompare emitter analogues and binding-pocket variants using product analysis, spectra, photon yield and reaction kinetics, not color alone.

14 / Light & photochemistryEstablished

Spin-selective oxygen photochemistry

Excited oxygen generation and reaction pathways

Photosensitized oxidation in cells and light-induced molecular damage.

Two views of the mechanism

Different levels of description. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [28]

Sensitizer excitation → energy transfer → reactive oxygen chemistry

How the mechanism works

Oxygen’s electronic state affects its chemistry. A sensitizer can absorb light and transfer energy to oxygen, populating a reactive excited state. Competition between emission, quenching, diffusion and substrate reaction determines the local outcome. “Reactive oxygen species” is a category, not a single spin state. [28]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Time-resolved photosensitization experiments in human skin fibroblasts characterize singlet-oxygen kinetics in a cellular environment. These studies link molecular photophysics to spatially and temporally constrained oxidation. [28]

Where the evidence stops

Observed oxygen photochemistry does not establish that ordinary weak magnetic fields control cellular health. Nor does a measured singlet-oxygen yield by itself establish therapeutic safety or efficacy. [28]

Physical effectStrong for photosensitized chemistry
Biological roleCellular oxidation demonstrated
Advantage / adaptationAdaptive interpretation not required

Real-world relevance

Established photochemical basis

Relevant to photodynamic strategies, phototoxicity testing and photostability. Translation requires a defined sensitizer, light dose, oxygen availability and indication-specific evidence; this atlas does not assess any treatment.

15 / Genome & repairSupported

DNA charge transport and repair search

Transport demonstrated; search role still scoped

DNA-mediated redox chemistry and lesion-search models for selected repair proteins.

Two views of the mechanism

Additional search channel, not replacement of diffusion. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [32][33]

DNA stacking + redox cofactors → proposed long-range coordination

How the mechanism works

An electronic charge can move through DNA by mechanisms that depend on sequence, stacking, energetics and distance. Short-range tunneling and longer-range multistep transfer are not interchangeable. Some repair proteins carry redox cofactors that could use DNA-mediated electron exchange to redistribute on a strand. [32][33]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

Experiments support DNA-mediated charge transport and redox interactions of repair proteins. Follow-up studies tested a model in which lesion-induced transport interruption helps focus a search. [32][33]

Where the evidence stops

These findings do not establish a universal DNA “wire network” in a living nucleus. The relative contribution compared with diffusion and sliding, and the physiological redox partners, must be established for each system. [32][33]

Physical effectStrong for transport in prepared systems
Biological roleRepair-search contribution supported, bounded
Advantage / adaptationGeneral physiological advantage unresolved

Laboratory sensing and biological research

Motivates DNA-based electrochemical sensors and mechanistic repair studies. A sensor can exploit DNA transport even if a proposed in-vivo search function remains incomplete.

16 / Genome & repairHypothesis

DNA tautomerization and mutation

Tunneling contribution to mutations is unquantified

Alternative protonation and tautomeric states of nucleobases.

Two views of the mechanism

Additional microscopic route within an error model. Original schematic, not a molecular structure, trajectory or measured dataset. [15][16][60]

Rare state → copying event → misincorporation → escape from repair

How the mechanism works

A change in proton position can change hydrogen-bonding preferences. Quantum nuclear motion may contribute to accessing those configurations. Mutation, however, is a chain of events: rare-state formation, survival, polymerase incorporation and failure of proofreading or repair. [15][16]

What has actually been measured or modeled?

NMR and kinetic modeling connect rare states of mismatched bases with misincorporation. Separate open-system calculations predict tunneling contributions in modeled guanine–cytosine pairs. These are complementary but do not measure one continuous tunneling-to-mutation pathway in a cell. [15][16][60]

Where the evidence stops

The modeled canonical G–C transfer problem and the experimentally probed mismatched-base states are not the same experiment. A predicted tautomer population cannot be reported as the fraction of human mutations caused by tunneling. [15][16][60]

Physical effectRare base states measured; tunneling modeled
Biological roleTunneling-to-mutation chain incomplete
Advantage / adaptationNo quantified organism-level contribution

Real-world relevance

Mechanistic hypothesis

Could refine mutation models and polymerase fidelity studies. It does not currently justify a “quantum mutation” diagnostic, cancer-prevention intervention or deterministic prediction of individual mutations.

A discriminating next test · proposedCombine isotope-sensitive state measurements with polymerase assays and mutation sequencing, controlling replication speed, damage, selection and repair.

Scope, grading method and limits of coverage

This is a broad, curated atlas of major mechanism families, not a systematic review or a literal inventory of every proposed quantum effect in biology. It includes established quantum chemistry where useful, narrower nonclassical dynamics, disputed native mechanisms and engineered protein platforms. It groups related enzyme families and separates overlapping claims when their evidence differs.

Evidence is graded for the specific claim using the cited original experiments and theoretical papers. A purified sample, a model, an intact-cell measurement and a behavioral endpoint are explicitly different. Negative and challenging studies are included where they materially affect interpretation. “Established” means strong support within scope—not philosophical proof or automatic evolutionary advantage.

Application paragraphs distinguish documented mechanism-level relevance from prospective design implications; they are not independent product-performance evaluations. Suggested next tests are original proposals, not experiments reported as completed. No clinical recommendations are made. Magnetite-based sensing, induction and ordinary receptor binding appear as alternatives rather than being relabeled as exotic quantum biology.

03 / From physics to practice

What changes in the real world?

A mechanism can be useful without proving that evolution selected a uniquely quantum advantage. Application maturity is its own question.

Quantum biology

A quantum-mechanical feature contributes to a biological process. Example: electron transfer in a redox protein. The biological function is the object of study. [4][5]

Quantum technology for biology

A deliberately introduced or engineered quantum system measures or controls biology. Diamond nanothermometry is an example; it does not mean the cell evolved a diamond quantum sensor. [56]

04 / A history of ideas and tests

From “What is life?”
to measurements inside cells.

The history is not a straight line of confirmed predictions. Experimental tools repeatedly turn broad ideas into narrower, testable claims.

1932–1933Ideas

Questions before mechanisms

Jordan and Bohr ask how quantum physics relates to living matter. These conceptual arguments precede the mechanistic experiments that define the modern field. [57][58]

1944Ideas

Molecular stability and heredity

Schrödinger’s What Is Life? links physical stability with hereditary information and an “aperiodic crystal.” It is not a discovery of DNA structure or evidence for long-lived biological coherence. [59]

1948Theory

Energy transfer becomes calculable

Förster develops resonance-transfer theory. Its familiar population-rate limit is quantum-derived—a useful reminder that effective classical dynamics can contain quantum physics. [29]

1956Theory

The environment enters electron transfer

Marcus relates electron-transfer rates to electronic coupling, thermodynamic driving force and molecular reorganization. [6]

1963Hypothesis

A quantum route to genetic change?

Löwdin proposes biological consequences of proton tunneling in DNA. The modern question remains how to connect microscopic transfer with copying and repair. [60]

1966Experiment

Tunneling evidence in a biological redox reaction

DeVault and Chance study cytochrome oxidation in Chromatium. Low-temperature behavior supports a tunneling interpretation rather than simple over-barrier activation. [61]

1968Hypothesis

Driven collective vibrations

Fröhlich proposes long-range energy organization in driven biological modes. Mode condensation and genuine quantum coherence will later need careful separation. [48]

1974Theory

Thermally activated tunneling in proteins

Hopfield develops an electron-transfer description in which nuclear/environmental motion and tunneling cooperate, rather than treating warmth as an automatic quantum veto. [62]

1978Hypothesis

The radical-pair compass proposal

Schulten, Swenberg and Weller describe how field-modulated electron-spin dynamics could provide a sensory mechanism. [63]

1989–2002Experiment

Enzyme tunneling becomes experimentally concrete

Isotope and temperature experiments in selected enzymes make nuclear tunneling a testable contribution to chemical kinetics, not a generic explanation of all catalysis. [21][5]

1991–1992Experiment

Ultrafast vision and protein electron transfer

Rhodopsin’s initial photochemistry is resolved on femtosecond timescales, while systematic protein studies characterize electron-transfer design constraints. [3][4]

2000–2002Debate

Brain-coherence models face constraints

Decoherence estimates and counter-models disagree about relevant assumptions. This is an important theoretical debate, not experimental proof for either a quantum consciousness mechanism or every possible impossibility claim. [19][20]

2007Experiment

Photosynthetic coherence moves center stage

Oscillatory FMO spectra are interpreted as wavelike energy transfer, creating a wave of interest and a need for more discriminating electronic versus vibrational assignments. [7]

2008–2009Experiment

Chemical compasses and helpful noise

An artificial radical-pair system demonstrates chemical-compass feasibility. Open-system transport models show that environmental noise can sometimes improve transfer. [13][11]

2011–2013Experiment

The scope broadens

DNA spin-selective transmission, olfactory isotope studies and nanoscale diamond thermometry illustrate very different meanings of “quantum and biology”: native mechanism, disputed sensory proposal and an external research tool. [42][17][56]

2015–2018Debate

Stronger tests separate similar claims

Odorant-receptor experiments challenge vibrational smell. Polarization-controlled spectroscopy separates FMO coherence contributions. Rare DNA base-state measurements address a different claim from direct proton tunneling. [18][8][15]

2021Experiment

A specific avian protein candidate

Magnetic sensitivity is demonstrated in European-robin cryptochrome 4 in vitro. Establishing the whole sensory pathway remains a separate task. [14]

2023Debate

Replication and origins experiments

Positive and negative fly-magnetosensitivity studies highlight assay-dependent evidence. Separately, a magnetic-surface experiment provides a laboratory route to chiral enrichment of an RNA precursor. [38][39][53]

2024Experiment

Collective protein optics

Tryptophan-network modeling and optical measurements on protein assemblies support collective-emission interpretations. Cognitive function is not demonstrated by the optical experiment. [47]

2025Experiment

Protein qubits and renewed transport modeling

A fluorescent-protein qubit is coherently controlled under cryogenic conditions. Separately, detailed FMO simulations revisit persistent quantum effects; the two results answer very different questions. [54][9]

2026Experiment

An engineered, living-cell sensing frontier

MagLOV provides room-temperature spin-resonance readout in living bacteria. Artificial light-harvesting aggregates show functional vibronic effects. Neither result alone establishes a corresponding evolved natural function. [55][10]

A recurring lesson across the timeline: conceptual possibility, a molecular signal, a functional mechanism and an adaptive advantage are different milestones. New instruments can strengthen one claim while leaving the next one open.

02 / TUNNELING

A barrier need not
be crossed over.

For a particle below a finite potential barrier, a quantum wavefunction can extend through the barrier and support nonzero transmission. Energy conservation still holds; no temporary “energy loan” is required.[1]

Barrier width and particle mass enter the tunneling dependence strongly. Compare an electron, a proton and a deuteron while keeping the idealized barrier and incident energy fixed.

T ∝ exp[−2a√(2m(V₀ − E)) / ℏ]Leading exponential for a sufficiently opaque rectangular barrier.

Biological connection: replacing H with D changes nuclear mass. An isotope effect can help test tunneling, but changes in vibrations, reaction steps and geometry must also be considered.[5]

03 / SUPERPOSITION & COHERENCE

The probabilities match.
The interference does not.

A superposition has complex probability amplitudes with a relative phase. It is not merely uncertainty about which of two classical alternatives is occupied. Phase-sensitive measurements distinguish a coherent superposition from an incoherent mixture.[1]

Here both site populations are always 50%. Changing the relative phase alters a measurement in the superposition basis. Reducing coherence removes that phase sensitivity.

P(+) = ½ [1 + C cos φ]C = coherence contrast, from 0 to 1; φ = relative phase.

Do not equate: spatial delocalization, persistent oscillatory coherence and entanglement. They describe different properties, and a claim must specify which one is being tested.

The density matrix behind the picture

In the site basis A, B, the state used here is:

ρ = ½ [ [1, C e−iφ], [C eiφ, 1] ]

The diagonal terms remain ½. Off-diagonal terms encode coherence in this chosen basis. Measuring |+⟩ = (|A⟩ + |B⟩)/√2 yields the plotted probability. This is an algebraic two-state illustration, not a photosynthetic complex simulation.

04 / OPEN QUANTUM SYSTEMS

Warm and wet
does not settle the question.

A molecule interacts with solvent, nearby charges and vibrations. Those interactions can reduce accessible phase coherence. Losing phase coherence is not identical to losing excitation energy.[2]

Compare a chosen coherence lifetime T₂ with a reaction time. The useful question is whether the quantum feature persists long enough to change the event—not whether the organism is perfectly isolated.

Scale check: 1 femtosecond (fs) = 10⁻¹⁵ s; 1 picosecond (ps) = 1,000 fs. A short lifetime can still matter to an equally fast process.

Can noise ever help?

In some model networks, moderate environmental dephasing improves transport by relieving localization; excessive noise suppresses it. This has also been tested in engineered trapped-ion networks. Neither result alone demonstrates an evolved advantage in a living organism.[11][12]

Thermal preparation.

06 / SPIN & RADICAL PAIRS

A tiny field can
change a reaction's path.

Electron spin is intrinsic angular momentum, not a literal spinning ball. Two unpaired electrons can occupy singlet and triplet states. In suitable reactions, their spin evolution affects the balance of chemical products.[13]

|S⟩ = (|↑↓⟩ − |↓↑⟩) / √2The singlet is an entangled two-spin state.

There are three triplet states. One also combines opposite-spin basis states, but with a plus sign. “Opposite spins = singlet, parallel spins = triplet” is therefore an incomplete shortcut.

Biological connection: cryptochrome 4 from the European robin has magnetically sensitive photochemistry in vitro. That supports a candidate mechanism—not a complete demonstrated navigation pathway.[14]

Why compare a magnetic field with chemistry, not heat?

A radical-pair compass need not thermally align molecules against random motion. Light initiates a nonequilibrium reaction, and the field can alter spin-dependent branching. Molecular anisotropy and suitable organization are needed for directional information.

The field arrow here is an orientation schematic. It does not calculate spin populations, reaction yield, receptor sensitivity or a bird's perceived direction. A realistic prediction needs a spin Hamiltonian, hyperfine couplings, kinetics and environmental parameters.

06 / A closer look at the debate

Photosynthesis is the lesson
in why evidence needs context.

An oscillation is a finding. Assigning it to a mechanism is an interpretation. Showing that the mechanism improves natural function is a further experiment.

2007
Experiment / FMO complex

A striking signal

Ultrafast spectroscopy reported oscillations interpreted as evidence for wavelike excitation-energy transfer through quantum coherence.[7]

2018
Experiment / signal assignment

Which coherence?

Polarization-controlled measurements assigned long-lived FMO oscillations to vibrational coherence, with electronic coherence decaying within 240 fs at 77 K.[8]

2025
Microscopic simulation

A model-based challenge

Detailed calculations predicted persistent excitonic coherences at 77 K and room temperature, highlighting sensitivity to how vibrations are modeled.[9]

2026
Experiment / artificial aggregates

Feasibility, not universality

Room-temperature vibronic coupling was reported in artificial porphyrin nanotubes. These are light-harvesting materials, not living plants.[10]

The careful conclusion: quantum excitation physics is indispensable. The lifetime, identity and functional benefit of a particular coherence are separate, system-specific questions. One signal does not prove that photosynthesis “computes every route at once.”

07 / Test the inference

A molecular possibility
is not yet a biological outcome.

DNA provides a useful example. Proton transfer is only one link in a chain that would need to be established to explain a mutation.

LINK 01

A rare state forms

A base changes its proton arrangement or ionization. Models can test a tunneling contribution.[16]

LINK 02

The state persists

Its lifetime and population must be relevant when a copying enzyme encounters it.

LINK 03

Copying is altered

A mismatch is incorporated. Experiments link rare base states with misincorporation mechanisms.[15]

LINK 04

The error remains

The copying error must escape correction and become fixed before it counts as a lasting mutation.

This chain is a framework for causal inference, not a measured sequence proving tunneling-driven mutation in cells. Evidence for rare states does not uniquely identify how they formed.

Four questions for any claim

1. What is explicitly quantum?

Identify a specific tunneling contribution, off-diagonal coherence, entangled state or spin-dependent reaction. “Quantum” alone is not a mechanism or a testable explanation.

2. What alternatives were ruled out?

An isotope effect may reflect several rate-limiting steps. An optical oscillation may be vibrational. Test the proposed quantum interpretation against realistic competitors, not an artificially weak classical model.

3. Was biological function measured?

Connect the molecular measurement to a meaningful endpoint. Perturb the proposed mechanism selectively, control folding, binding and expression, and use an independent perturbation or rescue where possible.

4. What establishes an advantage?

A quantum effect can occur without explaining rate enhancement or adaptive benefit. Define a realistic comparator and distinguish a causal contribution from evidence of selection.

08 / The vocabulary

A compact quantum lexicon.

Open a term for a working definition. These distinctions prevent many common misunderstandings.

Amplitude

A complex number whose magnitude squared contributes to a measurement probability. Relative phases allow interference.

Coherence

A phase relationship represented by off-diagonal density-matrix elements in a specified basis. The basis matters.

Decoherence

Loss of accessible phase relationships through interaction with an environment. It need not equal immediate energy loss.

Delocalization

A state extends over multiple sites. Spatial extent does not, by itself, establish long-lived oscillatory coherence.

Entanglement

A joint state that cannot be written as a statistical mixture of independent product states. Mere classical correlation is not enough.

Exciton

A description of electronic excitation in a coupled system; excitation can be distributed across molecular sites.

Radical pair

Two radicals, each with an unpaired electron, whose spin state can influence chemical reaction outcomes.

Reorganization energy

The energy scale associated with rearranging molecular and environmental coordinates for electron transfer.

Tautomer

An alternative form of a molecule with a changed proton position and associated bonding arrangement.

Tunneling

Quantum transmission through a potential-energy barrier inaccessible to an otherwise equivalent classical particle at that energy.

Vibronic coupling

Interaction or mixing between electronic and vibrational degrees of freedom.

In vitro / in vivo

In vitro: studied outside an intact organism in a controlled setting. In vivo: studied within a living organism. Neither label alone describes evidence quality.

PCET

Proton-coupled electron transfer: electronic and protonic changes interact energetically. The steps may be concerted or sequential; the particles need not travel the same distance.

CISS

Chirality-induced spin selectivity: a relationship between handedness and spin-dependent electronic behavior in a specified molecular platform. Native biological function is a separate question.

Superradiance

Collectively enhanced radiative behavior under appropriate preparation and coupling. A fluorescence change alone does not establish a cognitive function.

Internal conversion

A nonradiative transition between electronic states, transferring energy into nuclear motion. The detailed pathway depends on molecular geometry and environment.

ODMR

Optically detected magnetic resonance: a resonance-driven change in spin populations or dynamics is read through an optical signal.

Adaptive advantage

A biological improvement attributable to a trait in a relevant evolutionary setting. It is not established merely by observing a physical effect.

Nuclear quantum effects

Effects of treating nuclei quantum mechanically, including zero-point motion, delocalization and tunneling. These are not synonymous with long-lived coherence.

Nonadiabatic dynamics

Dynamics in which a single electronic potential-energy surface is insufficient because transitions or mixing between electronic states matter.
